The project

The problem was not a lack of tools. It was having to check too many places.

Running a small studio still creates a lot to keep track of: businesses to research, conversations to remember, follow-ups to complete, projects to deliver, invoices to watch and available capacity to understand.

Generic CRMs can cover parts of that work, but I would still be moving between sales tools, inboxes, project notes and spreadsheets. MateOS started with a simpler question: what do I need in one place to run Mate Code Studio day to day?

The challenge

Research, client context and delivery can quickly end up in separate places.

The problem is usually a collection of small misses: a business that never gets reviewed, a follow-up that slips, a project that becomes overloaded, an invoice that needs attention or a conversation that has to be reconstructed from memory.

The goal

Give Mate Code Studio one place to see what needs attention from first contact to active delivery.

It needed enough structure to support daily work without turning into a miniature enterprise platform.

  • Research and review potential businesses
  • Keep client and contact context together
  • Surface due and overdue work
  • See delivery capacity before taking on more work
  • Keep payment and operational risks visible

The solution

A practical internal workspace that connects the parts of the business I use every day.

MateOS connects business research, CRM records, follow-ups, active projects, capacity planning, payment visibility and recurring operational actions in one place.

The point is not to have more modules. It is to keep the context from one stage of work available when the next stage begins.

A deliberate decision

The dashboard starts with what needs attention.

The dashboard is not there to fill the screen with charts. It brings together the work, money and relationships that may need attention, with the relevant action close by.

Projects at risk, cash due, follow-ups, overdue actions, client messages, active work and businesses waiting for review are more useful to me than vanity metrics.

The same idea carries through the rest of MateOS: keep related information together and reduce the time spent working out what happened before.

What was built

The parts I use to run the studio day to day.

Each area exists because it solves a recurring part of the work. MateOS is not trying to reproduce a generic CRM feature-for-feature.

Decision-focused dashboard

Today, overdue work, project risk, cashflow signals, messages and review queues are surfaced in one action-oriented starting point.

Targeted discovery

Potential businesses can be researched in selected markets and kept separate from the CRM until they are worth reviewing further.

Lead review & prioritisation

Research can be filtered and reviewed before a business becomes an active CRM record, keeping early-stage information separate from real client relationships.

Client CRM

Prospects, leads, clients and past clients stay searchable with business context, lifecycle status and contact policy in one place.

Capacity planning

Weekly allocation, deadlines and a six-month availability view help protect delivery capacity before new work is committed.

Operational automation

Deterministic rules can create actions around events such as opportunity wins, waiting on clients, invoice reminders and payment chasing.

Workflow

From first research to active delivery, the context stays with the work.

MateOS follows the same basic path I use in the business: research a potential company, review it, move relevant relationships into the CRM, keep the next action visible and plan delivery once work becomes real.

Built with constraints

Built for one business, not for everyone.

MateOS is built for one operating environment: Mate Code Studio. Features are added because they solve a recurring operational problem, not because a generic CRM is expected to have them.

Research and discovery are separate from the core CRM. If new work comes through referrals, website enquiries or manual research, the rest of MateOS still works in the same way.

That keeps the MVP useful now and leaves room to add finance, delivery or automation features when daily use proves they are worth having.

The outcome

A working internal MVP that gives Mate Code Studio one place to manage opportunities, client work and day-to-day operations.

MateOS is not a SaaS product or a promise of automated growth. It is a working internal tool that gives me one place to understand relationships, see what needs attention and keep delivery organised.
